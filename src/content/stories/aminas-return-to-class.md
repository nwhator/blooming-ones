For fourteen-year-old Amina, attending public junior secondary school in the bustling Agege district of Lagos used to mean calculating calendar days with dread. Without access to affordable sanitary pads or supportive facilities, the onset of her menstrual cycle regularly forced her to stay home for up to five days each month.

> "I used to feel intense shame whenever my period arrived," Amina recalls quietly. "I had to use scrap cloth that leaked, and if boys laughed, you wanted the earth to open up. Missing school meant missing tests and falling behind in mathematics."

### The Reality of Period Poverty

Amina’s experience is far from isolated. Across low-income communities in Nigeria, menstrual poverty remains one of the primary drivers of adolescent female dropout and classroom disengagement. When families must choose between feeding the household and purchasing disposable sanitary products, menstrual care is often deprioritized.

In September 2024, The Blooming Ones Initiative (then operating under our founding banner, The Testybam Initiative) launched a targeted Menstrual Dignity Clinic at Amina’s school. Beyond delivering comprehensive kits containing six months of biodegradable pads, antibacterial soap, and underwear, our volunteer healthcare mentors sat with girls to dismantle long-held cultural taboos.

### From Secrecy to Confidence

Through our interactive workshops, Amina learned that menstruation is a natural biological milestone—not an affliction or something to be kept in shame. Armed with dignity materials and practical menstrual hygiene management education, Amina hasn't missed a single school day over the past academic year.

Today, Amina serves as a peer ambassador in her classroom, helping younger girls navigate puberty with self-assurance.

> "Now when my period comes, I walk tall. The Blooming Ones showed us that our bodies are worthy of care and respect."
